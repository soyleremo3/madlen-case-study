MagicSchool AI: the global specialist. MagicSchool is the category leader and Madlen's closest product analogue. After a $45M Series B (Feb 2025) it reports 6M+ educators across 160 countries, with 80+ teacher tools, 50+ student tools and district dashboards. Strengths: reach and trust. It has a free-forever plan (Plus from $8.33 a month), SOC 2/FERPA/COPPA compliance, a public pledge not to train AI on teacher or student data, and standards pulled from live databases rather than model memory. Weaknesses: these come with its scale. Independent reviews cite slow loading at peak hours, template-bound tools, levelled texts that miss the target grade, and no export to Docs, Word or the LMS on the free plan. Its 80+ single-purpose generators feel like a menu, not a workflow. It is also US-first: standards come from US databases and the interface has no Turkish. Turkish output relies on machine translation that MagicSchool itself says may contain errors, and nothing maps to MEB Maarif outcomes or LGS/YKS formats.

Google Gemini in Classroom: the free default. Since June 2025 Google has given every Google Workspace for Education account 30+ free AI tools: lesson outlines, quizzes, rubrics, re-levelled texts and misconception support. It also offers student-facing Gems, Gemini Notebook study guides and class analytics, inside the Classroom that many Turkish private schools already use daily. Strengths: zero cost, zero onboarding and Google's brand. Weaknesses: it is a horizontal AI layer, not a teaching system. Google itself warns that "Gemini can make mistakes" and asks teachers to refine every output to fit "your context and local policies", so the localisation work stays with the teacher. We found no published alignment to MEB Maarif learning outcomes, LGS/YKS item formats or IB/Cambridge programmes. It only reaches schools on Google Workspace (not Microsoft 365 or K12NET), and access depends on the school's IT admin.

Where Madlen stands

  • Madlen cannot win on price or tool count. Google is free, and MagicSchool has a free tier and more tools.
  • Madlen wins where rivals are structurally weak. It is local-first: MEB Maarif (TYMM) outcome mapping, LGS/TYT/AYT, IB and Cambridge, Turkish-native, and KVKK with EU-hosted data. It is also a connected loop: plan, create, assign, mark, track and report to parents and admins in one system.
  • Evidence that the loop matters: 98% of questions created in Madlen were assigned to students without a single edit (233,702 of 238,281). In a 2026 randomised trial, Khanmigo's AI tutor, which sat beside the content rather than inside it, saw "thin" engagement. Sal Khan's conclusion: the AI "had to be woven into" the content.

Madlen is the AI teaching assistant built for your curriculum, not adapted to it: lessons, questions and feedback arrive aligned to your learning outcomes (MEB Maarif, LGS/YKS, IB or Cambridge), ready to assign, with the teacher always in control. Because planning, assigning, marking and tracking happen in one connected loop, teachers win back hours and school leaders see real learning progress, not just activity.

Short version for ads and social: "Built for your curriculum. Ready for your class."

Who it is for

The teacher uses it every day and cares about time and output they can trust without rewriting. The school leader buys it and cares about measurable progress, curriculum compliance and data safety (KVKK). The first sentence speaks to the teacher; the second speaks to the leader.

Answering the case's question

Is Madlen's advantage time-saving, data insights or meaningful learning? All three, in a specific order. Time saving gets the teacher in the door, but every rival offers it. Curriculum fit makes the output trustworthy enough to use, and rivals cannot match it locally. The connected loop turns that use into learning data leaders can act on, which is the proof of meaningful learning. The UVP leads with curriculum fit because that is the part competitors cannot copy quickly.

Deliberately not claimed: cheapest, most tools or biggest. "With the teacher always in control" was added after reading Madlen's own stance ("her çıktı öğretmen kontrolünden geçiyor") and MEB's human-oversight principle. "Ready to assign" means classroom-ready quality, not "no need to check".

Step 3 · Strategic initiative: Madlen Ethics Desk

Product development with a launch plan: make MEB's new AI ethics rules the easiest part of using AI with students.

The insight

  • New rules bind every school. MEB's AI Ethics directive (22 Oct 2025) requires every school, private ones included, to form an AI Ethics Team led by the principal. It oversees AI use; a violating application is stopped.
  • The paperwork starts exactly where Madlen is different. Since January 2026, teachers must file an ethics declaration on YAZEK whenever AI interacts with students, processes student work or supports assessment. Lesson prep needs no form. Free tools compete on that paperwork-free part; Madlen's connected loop (assigning, student chat, essay feedback) adds a compliance task at every step.
  • The gap today. Madlen explains these rules well in its blog. On its public product pages we found no in-product support for writing declarations or for the ethics team's oversight work.

What we build (one term)

Declaration draft in one click (teacher). When a teacher assigns a student-facing activity, Madlen pre-fills the declaration fields: purpose, tool, linked learning outcome, where data is stored (EU) and how each ethics principle is met. The teacher reviews it and submits it on YAZEK. Madlen never submits on the teacher's behalf.
Ethics Team view (principal, in the existing admin panel). An inventory of every student-facing AI activity with its declaration status, a human-oversight trail (feedback approved before students saw it), and a one-click summary for meetings and parent questions.

Launch: principal webinars ("Set up your AI Ethics Team in one hour"), a starter kit (role letter, teacher announcement, parent FAQ), and the Ethics Desk as the headline of the 14-day school pilot.

How it reinforces the UVP

"Built for your curriculum" becomes "built for your rules": the framework is Turkish, so US-first or horizontal rivals are unlikely to build YAZEK workflows soon. The connected loop becomes a compliance advantage: one platform, one audit trail. The principal (the buyer) sees learning progress and governance in one panel.

Objective

  • Remove the friction that keeps teachers in prep-only use, so more use the student-facing loop (Madlen's differentiator).
  • Win the buyer: a concrete reason for principals to choose one compliant platform over free scattered tools.
  • Build trust in Madlen as the responsible-AI partner of Turkish schools.

Try it in two minutes

Lesson prep: pick an example chip (e.g. "Photosynthesis", grade 7), press "Plan my lesson", then "Create a 5-question quiz" and tick "Show answer key".

Study helper: send Solve: 3x + 5 = 20 and ask for the answer twice: it gives hints, not the answer. Then ask Write 300 words about Atatürk: it gives an outline, not an essay.

Essay feedback: press "Try a sample essay", get feedback, change a score, remove a note, then approve and copy the student summary.
