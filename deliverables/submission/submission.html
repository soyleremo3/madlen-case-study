1. Competitive analysis

I looked at six AI tools a school in Türkiye could use instead of Madlen (MagicSchool, Gemini in Classroom, Khanmigo, MEBİ, Oak Aila and ChatGPT for Teachers). I chose the two a school is most likely to compare with Madlen: a global specialist and a free tool that is already installed.

MagicSchool AI

MagicSchool is the market leader and the product closest to Madlen. It raised a $45M Series B in February 2025 and reports 6 million educators in 160 countries. Its strengths are breadth and trust: 80+ teacher tools and 50+ student tools, a free plan (Plus costs $8.33 a month), SOC 2, FERPA and COPPA compliance, a promise not to train AI on user data, and standards pulled from live databases. Its weaknesses show up in daily use. Independent reviews mention slow loading at peak hours, rigid templates, levelled texts that miss the target grade and no export on the free plan. With 80 separate generators, the teacher has to connect the steps alone. For Türkiye the bigger gap is localisation. The interface has no Turkish, Turkish output is machine-translated (MagicSchool itself warns it may contain errors), and nothing maps to the Maarif curriculum or to LGS/YKS.

Google Gemini in Classroom

Since June 2025 every Google Workspace for Education account gets Gemini in Classroom for free. It has 30+ tools for lesson outlines, quizzes, rubrics and levelled texts, plus study guides for students and class analytics. Its strengths are that it costs nothing, needs no setup, carries Google's brand and sits inside the Classroom that many private schools in Türkiye already use. Its weakness is that it is a general-purpose AI added to Classroom. Google tells teachers that "Gemini can make mistakes" and asks them to adapt every output to "your context and local policies", so the local work stays with the teacher. I found no published alignment to Maarif outcomes, LGS/YKS formats or IB/Cambridge. It also only reaches schools on Google Workspace, and the school's IT admin decides who can use it.

Where Madlen stands

Madlen cannot win on price or on the number of tools. It can win on two things neither rival has: content built on the curricula Turkish schools teach (Maarif outcomes, LGS/YKS, IB and Cambridge), and one system that connects planning, assigning, marking and reporting. Madlen's own data supports this: 98% of the questions teachers created in Madlen were assigned to students without a single edit (233,702 of 238,281).

2. Unique value proposition

Madlen is the AI teaching assistant built for your curriculum, not adapted to it: lessons, questions and feedback arrive aligned to your learning outcomes (MEB Maarif, LGS/YKS, IB or Cambridge), ready to assign, with the teacher always in control. Because planning, assigning, marking and tracking happen in one connected loop, teachers win back hours and school leaders see real learning progress, not just activity.

Teachers use Madlen every day and care about time and about output they can trust. School leaders pay for it and care about measurable progress and compliance. The first sentence speaks to the teacher and the second to the leader. It leads with curriculum fit because that is the gap both competitors leave open. "The teacher always in control" is Madlen's own position and an MEB requirement, so it belongs in the promise. I left out claims Madlen cannot win, such as cheapest or most tools.

3. Strategic initiative: Madlen Ethics Desk

Since January 2026, teachers in Türkiye must file an ethics declaration on MEB's YAZEK system whenever AI works with students: when it talks to them, processes their work or supports assessment. Since October 2025, every school also needs an AI ethics team led by the principal. Lesson prep needs no paperwork, but the parts that make Madlen different (assigning work, student chat, essay feedback) all do. Madlen explains these rules well on its blog, but I found nothing in the product that helps teachers or principals follow them.

The initiative is a product feature with two parts. When a teacher assigns a student-facing activity, Madlen fills in a draft of the YAZEK form from what it already knows: purpose, tool, linked learning outcome and where the data is stored. The teacher checks it and submits it; Madlen never submits on the teacher's behalf. Principals get a panel that lists every student-facing AI activity in the school with its declaration status, and records which AI feedback a teacher approved before students saw it.

I would launch it with a one-hour webinar for principals and make it the focus of Madlen's 14-day school pilot. It supports the UVP because it extends "built for your curriculum" to the local rules, which a US platform or a general Google layer is unlikely to build soon. It also gives the principal, who makes the purchase, a reason to choose one platform over several free tools.

Objective

Move more teachers from lesson prep into student-facing use, and give principals a concrete reason to buy.

4. AI-powered mini products

Live at kalem-case-study.vercel.app. No login is needed, and the interface works in English and Turkish.

Lesson prep is for teachers. Given a topic and a grade, it writes a lesson plan with objectives, key concepts, five slides with a visual idea for each, and discussion questions. It can also make a five-question quiz with an answer key.

The Study helper is for students. It adjusts its language to the student's grade, and on practice problems it gives hints step by step so the student reaches the answer.

Essay feedback is for teachers. It scores an essay on four criteria, attaches notes with rewrite examples to exact sentences, and drafts a summary for the student. The teacher edits and approves it before anything is shared.

To try it quickly, ask the Study helper to solve 3x + 5 = 20, or press "Try a sample essay" in Essay feedback. The process document is on the last page.

5. Social media post

I chose an Instagram carousel (five slides, 4:5). Madlen's Instagram audience is teachers, and carousels are the format people save and share. The post introduces Essay Marking. Marking a class set of essays is one of the biggest time costs for teachers, and the tool shows the UVP in practice: Madlen drafts the feedback and the teacher decides. The slides show 32 essays on a Sunday evening, the time that takes (32 × 10 min = 5 h 20 min), Madlen drafting feedback, the teacher approving it, and an invitation to try it free.
